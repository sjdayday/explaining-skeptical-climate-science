Two and a half beats don't make a rhythm

L&S11 pinned their long cycle at exactly 60 years, on astronomical grounds. But their calibration window — 1850–1950 — holds fewer than two repeats of such a cycle. If 60 were a real constant of nature, it should fit the data better than its neighbours. Turn the dial and see.

What this exercise shows. The R² landscape is broad and nearly flat — many periods fit the calibration window about equally well, and 60 is not the winner. On this NOAA snapshot the best long period is ≈87 years; on 2010-vintage HadCRUT3, Benestad et al. found 65.75 beat 60; and L&S11's own supplement concedes that a free fit centers near 65 — they imposed 60 anyway to match the astronomy. A real physical constant doesn't move when you switch thermometer networks, and it doesn't lose to its neighbours on the very data used to establish it. What you just felt with the slider is the sound of a window too short to pin down a rhythm: 100 years of data holds fewer than two beats of a 60-year cycle.
