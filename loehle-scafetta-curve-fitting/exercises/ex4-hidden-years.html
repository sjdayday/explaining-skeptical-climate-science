Flexible models must face data they've never seen

With four parameters, the joke goes, you can fit an elephant. L&S11's model had six — and was never tested on data it hadn't already touched. Here you get the same temptation they had: a knob that makes the past fit better and better. The future is behind a curtain.

The game: the real NOAA temperature record is shown up to 2010 — the eve of the paper's publication. Everything after is hidden. Your model is the claim's recipe, a straight line plus waves, and the knob sets how many waves you may use. Crank it until the past is memorized. Then commit, lift the curtain, and grade your forecast against what the planet actually did.

What this exercise shows. More waves always memorize the past better — the amber score can only go up. Prediction is another matter: past the first knob positions, extra waves latch onto wiggles that were never going to repeat, and the forecast degrades. Training-fit quality is cheap; it is the only currency L&S11 ever paid in. Their wiggle-match through 2010 reused years that had already shaped the model, and the pre-1950 “nature-only” premise assumed the small early human effect it set out to prove. The only clean exam is data the model never touched — which for them began in 2011. For the record: rebuilt on this data, their own published recipe misses the 2011–2025 years by ≈0.3 °C on average, and by ≈0.7 °C in 2024.
Before you lift the curtain: notice the feeling. Each extra wave makes the curve hug the past more lovingly, and the R² readout applauds. This is exactly the seduction of curve fitting — nothing in the amber score warns you that you are memorizing, not understanding. Commit to a knob setting, then face the future.

Honest labels. The temperature series is real: NOAA NOAAGlobalTemp (GCAG) annual anomalies, embedded at build time — the same snapshot as the case file. The model family is a straight line plus k sine waves whose periods are the fitting window's length (160 years) divided by 1, 2, …, k, all fitted by least squares on 1850–2010; the average-error grade is the root-mean-square miss against observed 2011–2025. The “L&S11 recipe” figures re-run their two-stage procedure on this data (cycles calibrated 1850–1950 plus the residual ramp), as in the case file's Exhibit E. L&S11's model is a hand-picked member of this same line-plus-waves family; the knob generalizes their choice.
